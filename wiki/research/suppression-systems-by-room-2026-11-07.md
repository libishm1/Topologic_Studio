# Suppression systems by room use: clean agent (HFC-227ea), foam, water and portable extinguishers (research, 2026-11-07)

Status: research note for the build that assigns a suppression system to each room by its name or use and adds two solver mechanisms (a room flooded with clean agent; a liquid pool blanketed with foam). It answers the user's request of 2026-11-07: water sprinklers in apartments, powder extinguishers at strategic places, HFC-227ea (FM-200) in electrical and server rooms of commercial buildings, foam in generator rooms and similar, tested on the commercial building types. It builds on `sprinkler-codes-2026-11-07.md` (how codes are cited; NFPA 13 hazard classes of rooms, its section 3.6), `extinguishers-2026-11-07.md` (SBC 801-CR-2018 Section 906 and NFPA 10 placement, its sections 1.1 to 1.3), `water-by-fire-class-2026-11-07.md` (water on class B, C and lithium-ion fires, its sections 5, 6 and 9), `fire-suppression-sprinklers-2026-11-07.md` and `sprinkler-physics-2026-11-07.md`; those notes are cited, not repeated.

How sources were handled. SBC 801-CR-2018 was read in the user's licensed copy, which stays outside the repository: Sections 603.3.2, 608, 903.2, 903.2.8, 903.3.1.1.1, 903.3.1.1.2, 903.3.1.2, 903.3.2, 904 (all of it), 906.1 and Table 906.1, 5704.3.7.5 and Chapter 80; only clause numbers and values appear here. NFPA 2001 was read in three forms: the 2004 edition in full (the Public.Resource.Org incorporation-by-reference copy at archive.org); the 2022 edition through NFPA's public First Draft report of its cycle (First Revisions of 2019, which print the revised clauses and tables in full) and Errata 2001-22-1; and the 2025 edition through the First and Second Revision reports of its cycle (2023 and 2024). NFPA 11, 75, 76, 37, 110 and 10 were read in NFPA's public draft or public-input reports on docinfofiles.nfpa.org, which print the clauses of the edition then in force with the committee's revisions; their numbers may differ by one revision from the published edition. No NFPA account was created, no copy of a standard hosted elsewhere was used, and ISO 14520-9, UL 162, EN 1568-3, ICAO Doc 9137 and the IFC 2021 were not read (secondary sources, marked). Two research agents gathered the foam values (section 3) and the room clauses of NFPA 75, 76, 37, 110, 855, 30 and IFC 2021 (sections 2 and 4); their key values were checked here against the downloaded texts (NFPA 75-2020 9.3 and 9.4.2, NFPA 10 5.5.4.6 and its annex, NFPA 11-2021 Table 5.7.3.2 and 6.5.2.2.1, and the foam test tables of Back and Farley 2020, where one of the agent's times was corrected). Every DOI in section 6 was resolved at api.crossref.org on 2026-11-07. Web text was treated as data. Values with two independent sources are marked "yes", with one "partly".

## As implemented (2026-11-07)

Built on `next/browser-core` and left uncommitted for the user's review; the interface is the user's to wire from the API below. Nothing runs, and no field exists, unless a run passes `systems`.

Rules and plan (`src/core/suppressionSystems.js`).

- `systemForRoom(room, { occupancy, sprinklered = true, agentOnly = false })` returns `{ system: "water" | "clean-agent" | "foam" | "none", use, reason, clause, sprinklers, design, extinguisher }`. Names are matched case-insensitively as whole words, the first rule that matches deciding: generator, genset, diesel, fuel, oil, day tank, flammable (and the Clinic's "FLAMABLE"), hazardous storage ("HAZ. STOR."), paint store, solvent, emergency power and engine room get foam; server, computer (not a computer lab or classroom, which is a room of desks), data centre or processing, ADP, NOC, network operations, IT room and MDF, IDF or BDF get the clean agent (use "it"), as do TELE, telecom, COMM and communications ("telecom"), UPS, BATT and battery ("battery"), and ELEC, electrical, switchgear, transformer and substation ("electrical"); elevator machine or equipment rooms, kitchens (whole word, so the Clinic's "KITCHENET" is not one) and mechanical or plant rooms (MECH, plant, AHU, boiler, pump, chiller, HVAC) get water, and so does every other room. An occupancy of Group R (R, R-1 to R-4, residential, apartment, dwelling) gives water in every room whatever its name (SBC 801-CR-2018 903.2.8). A clean-agent room keeps its sprinklers (NFPA 75-2024 9.1.1 and 9.4.1; 903.3.1.1.1 preamble) unless `agentOnly` (the official's approval under 903.3.1.1.1 items 1 and 2, and 904.2). A space roomsFromSpaces could not make a room of (a roof, an outdoor vestibule, a sliver) gets none, and `sprinklered: false` leaves the water rooms with none.
- `planSystems(rooms, { occupancy, sprinklered, agentOnly, temperature = 20 })` returns each room with its volume and agent mass (section 1.6), the counts by system, the clauses cited and a short report. `specialExtinguishers(plan, rooms)` lists the units the special rooms need of their own, one each at the room (its centre 1 m over the floor, for the interface to snap to the door): CO2 at IT, telecom, electrical, battery and elevator machine rooms (no powder on electronics: NFPA 75-2020 9.3.2, NFPA 10 5.5.4.6.2), a class B unit at fuel rooms (Table 906.3(2)) and class K at kitchens (904.12.5); the ABC powder on the walking routes stays with `extinguishers.js` `layoutExtinguishers`, and apartments get one unit per dwelling (906.1 item 1), which is not placed.
- On the examples. NIBS Office, 99 spaces: 89 water; 9 clean agent (NOC, two ELEC RM, two TELE/COMM RM, two SERVER RM, A/V SERVER RM, UPS/BATT RM), 330 kg of HFC-227ea at 7.0 % and 20 °C in all; no room qualifies for foam; 1 none (an outdoor vestibule). NIBS Clinic, 269 spaces: 250 water; 10 clean agent (ADP EQUIP SPACE, COMPUTER ROOM, four COMM. ROOM, two ELEC. ROOM, two ELECTRICAL), 179 kg; 2 foam (HAZ. STOR., FLAMABLE STORAGE); 7 none (six roofs, and "ELEC. CL.", 0.31 m wide, which roomsFromSpaces does not take as a room). Duplex, 21 spaces: 20 water, 1 none (its roof).

In the solver (`flamescapes.js`, WGSL `kernels.js`, `flamescapesRun.js`, `flamescapesGpu.js`).

- A run passes `systems: { rooms: [{ id, name, system: "clean-agent" | "foam", footprint, floor, ceiling (roomsFromSpaces' records, or `cells`), params }] }`; rooms of other systems are left to the sprinklers. `systemsSetup` marks each room's cells (open cells whose centres are inside the footprint and between floor and ceiling; a cell in two rooms goes to the first), lays out its detectors, and keeps per room eight floats of state: the agent's concentration, the two extinguishing concentrations, the foam's share of evaporation left and its wetting share. One JavaScript function computes that state from the step's start time for both backends: the CPU calls it at the start of `chemistry`, the GPU writes it (32 bytes a room) before each step.
- Clean agent. In `chemistry`, a cell of a flooded room whose agent is at or above the extinguishing concentration of its vapour's fuel has no gas-phase combustion, and fuel whose neighbouring gas is at or above the class A value does not oxidise; pyrolysis and a liquid's evaporation go on, so vapour builds up while the agent holds, and when the flood ends hot fuel lights again (a test lets a 30 s hold end and the fire comes back; NFPA 2001 A.7.4.1). The agent is not carried by the flow: it holds over the room's cells only (the enclosure is taken as closed and tight, as NFPA 2001 and the interlocks of SBC 801-CR-2018 904.3.3 require), so it reaches no cell outside the room by construction. The doors in the model stay as the run set them, and gas that leaves through an open one carries no agent and may burn outside.
- Foam. In a foamed room a liquid fuel cell's evaporation is multiplied by the blanket's share left, which falls linearly from 1 at discharge to 0.1 at the control time and to 0 at the extinguishment time and stays 0 for the run (the blanket's drainage is not modelled). A `foamWet` stage after the spray (the WGSL `systemsWet`, one thread per cell) wets, while foam is applied, the first fuel cell down each of the room's columns with `WETTED_FRACTION` of the solution landing on its column, as sprinkler water does in "wetting" mode; liquids, metals and packs take none.
- Detection is read at the end of each frame (5 s), on both backends alike: a room is detected at the end of the frame in which each of its zones has a detector at or below the response, and discharges after its delay (the agent from then; the foam from then).
- Output. `out.systems.rooms[]`: id, name, system, cells, volume, detectors, zones, detectedAt, dischargeAt, manual, and for the agent design, extinguishing {A, B}, agentKg, floodedAt, holdEnds, reachesA and reachesB (when the rise reached each value); for the foam rate, controlAt, outAt, foamEnds, wetCells; `stats` on the CPU only (the agent's oxidation held back and flames stopped, the foam's evaporation held back and water on the fuel). Each frame carries `systems: { detected, discharged }`. The worker passes `payload.systems` through.

Cost when off. The CPU's chemistry tests a local for null once per fuel cell and once per cell with fuel vapour (the gas test sits behind the existing vapour test), and the `foamWet` stage returns on one null check; the GPU binds a 16-byte placeholder and branches on a uniform. Results are bit-identical with systems off, null, an empty list or water rooms only (a test), and the existing 349 tests pass unchanged. What the runs show, and what they do not:

- Water. Heads opened at 27 to 46 s in every seed and the heat released fell by 49 to 72 % in the rooms of everyday size. The first Clinic room chosen, a 55 m2 examination room, kept its fire under 350 kW and opened a head in only one seed of three within 180 s (no change), so the water case there was rerun in the 29 m2 conference room (`--water-area 30`); the first run is in the results file. In the Clinic's computer room one seed of three flashed over with three heads open, which is most of water's smaller mean effect there (28 %).
- Clean agent. Both zones saw smoke in the first frame (5 s) in every seed: the class C first item starts burning at 900 K and its smoke reaches both detectors of a 32 to 58 m2 room within one frame. The agent discharged at 35 s, reached 5.2 % at 42.4 s and stopped every flame in the room; the 1.3 to 1.5 MJ after acting is the frame in which it rose and vapour that left by the door and burnt outside. With the sprinklers kept (the default assignment) the result is the same: in the Office no head opened (the agent was first) and in the Clinic one opened without changing anything. The 600 s hold outlasts the 180 s runs, so no re-ignition is seen here; the unit test and the GPU check show it after a 30 s hold.
- Foam. With the app's heptane item the pool goes out by itself on this grid when its 30 s pilot stops (its own flame's heat to the surface is below the grid's resolution), so the foam, arriving at 45 s, found no pool to blanket and acted only as water on the room's class A contents, where it did less than an ordinary sprinkler (28 % against 41 %), whose spray also cools the gas and whose footprint reaches past the 9 m2 room. With the spill kept alight by the pilot (`--cases foam-spill`), the foam put the spill out (no vapour from it after 64.5 s, its fuel kept) and cut the heat released by 41 % against 26 % for water; about 20 class A fuel cells of the room that the spill had lit kept burning at 100 to 180 kW, falling, under the foam's water, which wets them as sprinkler water does and no more. Seeds 1 and 3 gave the same run in this small room. Neither model has a generator room; the Clinic's HAZ. STOR. stands in for a foam room, as the request allowed.

Departures from the plan and limits (more in section 5):

- The agent is not transported, and the doors stay as the run set them: a total flood presumes a closed enclosure, so the model gives the room the agent and gives the open door none. Closing a flooded room's doors on discharge (NFPA 2001's interlocks) would need the grid's solid cells to change mid-run.
- The agent neither cools the gas nor dilutes its oxygen (about 1.5 points of O2 at 7 %, section 1.4), and no HF is produced; it acts only through the extinguishing concentrations, which already include both mechanisms for the flame.
- Large pools on the 0.35 m grid give off more vapour than burns near them; with a 1 m2 heptane pool kept alight in a 5 x 4 m test room, the vapour already in the room kept burning at about 110 kW for minutes after the blanket was down (a 0.1 m2 spill went out within about a minute). The foam test uses the spill.
- The foam's 30 s delay is assumed, and its control and extinguishment times are derived from one test series (section 3.6).

## 1. Clean agent HFC-227ea (NFPA 2001)

### 1.1 Editions

SBC 801-CR-2018 904.10 requires clean-agent systems to follow NFPA 2001, and its Chapter 80 names the 2015 edition (NFPA 2001-15; likewise NFPA 11-10, 16-15, 110-13, 10-13). The values below are those of the 2022 and 2025 editions where read, with the 2004 text where the later clause was not printed in a draft report. The 2022 edition was reorganised: design moved to Chapter 7 (5.4 became 7.2, 5.6 became 7.4, 5.5.2 became 7.3.2), people to Chapter 4 (1.5.1.2 became 4.3.2), and alarms and time delays to Section 9.7. The class A rule, the class C factor of 1.35 and the >480 V clause were already in the 2018 text that the 2022 draft revises (the draft changes only their numbers and cross-references); whether the 2015 edition named by SBC 801 had the factor of 1.35 was not checked (the 2004 edition set class C equal to class A, 5.4.2.5).

The class A listing test (NFPA 2001 annex, after UL 2166) defines the MEC as the concentration at which all visible flame is out within 600 s of discharge, the fuel loses at most 15 g between 10 s and 600 s after discharge, and the fuel does not reignite when the enclosure is ventilated after the 600 s soak. The MEC is therefore a 10-minute criterion; at the design concentration (1.2 to 1.35 times the MEC) flaming goes out faster, but no standard value of the time was found. For combinations of fuels the fuel needing the greatest concentration governs (7.2.1.2).

Design values in use: 7.0 % (class C) for server and electrical rooms, 8.6 to 8.7 % where a flammable liquid is the hazard; all below the NOAEL of 9.0 %, so the room may be normally occupied (1.4).

### 1.3 Discharge, hold and leakage

- Discharge: 95 % of the MDC in at most 10 s for halocarbons (2004 5.7.1.2.1; the discharge time is the time to discharge 95 % of the agent mass needed for the MDC, 5.7.1.2.3). Specifications ask for a nominal 10 s (UH 21 22 00).
- Hold (FD 2022 7.4.1): at least 85 % of the adjusted MDC at the highest height of protected content for 10 min, or long enough for trained personnel to respond. The annex (A.7.4.1) gives the reason: a persistent ignition source (an arc, a hot surface, a deep-seated fire) can restart the fire once the agent has dissipated. Hold is verified by a door-fan enclosure integrity test with the annex's descending-interface model; Hetrick (2008) compared the sharp interface model (NFPA 2001 annex), the wide interface model (ISO 14520) and a thick-interface model against 34 full-scale tests with seven agents, with hold defined as a 15 % decay: measured hold times were about 10 % shorter than the sharp-interface prediction (summary only).
- Ventilation: forced-air systems, including self-contained recirculation, are shut down or closed automatically where their operation would prevent the concentration being reached and held (FR 2025 7.1.7); SBC 801-CR-2018 904.3.3 requires interlocks with fuel shutoffs, ventilation, door closers and dampers. NFPA 75-2024 9.4.2.1 and 9.4.5 add power-off to IT equipment and air-handling shutdown.

### 1.4 People

- Exposure: NOAEL 9.0 %, LOAEL 10.5 % (cardiac sensitisation in dogs), PBPK times in row 11; the rules of 4.3.2.4 in row 12.
- Alarm and delay: a pre-discharge alarm and a time delay long enough for evacuation (SR 2025 9.7; 2004 4.3.5.6.1); the delay serves only evacuation or preparing the hazard, never confirming a detector (2004 4.3.5.6.2 and 4.3.5.6.3); it may be omitted for fast-growth hazards (ignitible liquid storage, aerosol filling, paint booths) if a supervised lockout valve locks the system out while people are inside (9.7.2, 9.7.3, A.9.7.2). SBC 801-CR-2018 904.3.4 requires distinct audible and visible alarms and signs for pending discharge and, where a delay is needed to evacuate, a separate signal once discharge begins.
- Sequence in practice (UH and Metrolinx specifications; no code sets the numbers): first detector in either zone gives an alarm; a second detector in the other zone gives a second alarm, shuts down air handling and starts a 30 s delay (programmable up to 60 s); a "hold" (abort) station, held pressed, stops or holds the countdown (Metrolinx: holds at 10 s); manual release at the exit discharges immediately or after an optional delay and overrides abort (2004 4.3.5.3). Abort stations are constant-pressure switches near the exits (2004 4.3.5.3).
- Oxygen: at 7.0 % agent the oxygen falls from 20.9 % to 19.4 %, at 8.7 % to 19.1 % (derived; the agent does not act by oxygen depletion, Fike).

### 1.5 How it extinguishes; deep-seated fires; HF

- Mechanism. Zegers et al. (2000, NRL, read) measured counterflow and cup-burner extinction for four fluorinated ethanes, ten fluorinated propanes including HFC-227ea, four halons and inert agents. Taking CF4 and SF6 as inert references (extinction at a fixed sensible enthalpy per mole of oxygen, 267 kJ/mol O2 for heptane), the chemical contribution of fluorinated agents without bromine or iodine was at most 35 %; the dominant physical effect is added thermal mass. Effectiveness rises with the number of CF3 groups; in their kinetic modelling HFC-227ea is consumed mainly by thermal decomposition (HF elimination and C-C bond fission) rather than radical attack. Williams, L'Espérance and Fleming (2000) measured intermediate species in methane flames inhibited by HFC-227ea against the NIST mechanism (not read). Manufacturers state the same split in words: mainly heat absorption, with some interruption of the flame chemistry, not oxygen depletion (Fike data sheet).
- Deep-seated fires. The 2022 edition lists class A fuels subject to smouldering (deep-seated) combustion among the hazards on which clean agents may not be used without tests acceptable to the authority (FD 2022 4.2.2 item 5), because no minimum extinguishing concentration has been established by test for any clean agent (committee statement to FR-25 and FR-38). The definition and annex (FD 2022 3.3.38 and A.3.3.38; 2025 A.3.3.10, "deep-seated fire") explain: flaming combustion is put out at low concentration and stays out; smouldering inside a permeable, insulating fuel mass is not, and stops only when the fuel is cooled (water) or held long enough under inert gas for its temperature to fall below re-ignition. Closely spaced vertical panels, excelsior in boxes and shredded paper can need higher concentrations and long holds; wood cribs cannot represent grouped cables in underfloor voids. A peer-reviewed measurement of re-ignition after a clean agent hold was not found.
- HF. Kim and Su (2004, NRC Canada; abstract read) tested HFC-227ea in a simulated electronic equipment room with six steel cabinets: HF stayed below 100 ppm in an open cabinet with a cable fire and reached 800 ppm with the cabinet closed (extinction took longer); with a heptane pool fire the room peaked at 3900 ppm. HF scales with the fire's size and the time to extinguish it, hence the 10 s discharge. NFPA 2001 (FD 2022 Annex B.1.2) gives 10-min emergency values of 170 ppm (ERPG-3), 50 ppm (ERPG-2) and 2 ppm (ERPG-1), and notes irritation that may impair escape above about 200 ppm for 2 min.

### 1.6 Quantity

W = (V / s) (C / (100 - C)), W in kg, V the net volume in m3, C the design concentration in % v/v, s = 0.1269 + 0.000513 t m3/kg at the minimum design temperature t (°C) (2004 5.5.1 and Table A.5.5.1(l); the footnote prints 0.0005, the tabled values follow 0.000513; ISO 14520-9 tables give 0.137 m3/kg at 20 °C and 0.1215 at -10 °C). At 20 °C: 0.524 kg/m3 at 6.7 %, 0.549 kg/m3 at 7.0 % (NFPA table 0.5483), 0.695 kg/m3 at 8.7 %. A 100 m3 server room at 7.0 % needs about 55 kg. The formula includes the normal efflux from a tight enclosure during discharge (2004 5.5.1.1). Altitude corrections are in Table 7.3.3.3 (FR 2025).

### 1.7 What the solver needs

The values, with the modelling choices marked as such:

1. Concentration in the protected room: from 0 to C_d over t_d = 10 s (linear ramp, or C_d (1 - exp(-t/3.34 s)), which reaches 95 % at 10 s; constructed), held at or above 0.85 C_d at the top of the contents until t_d + 600 s, then falling. The simplest decay consistent with the 85 % rule is well-mixed: C = C_d exp(-lambda (t - t_d)), lambda = ln(1/0.85)/600 s = 2.71e-4 /s, which reaches the class A MEC (5.2 %) from 7.0 % about 18 min after discharge (constructed; a descending interface keeps the upper room longer and empties the lower room first).
2. Flaming extinction: a cell with gas-phase combustion is extinguished while the local agent concentration is at least the MEC of its fuel (heptane 6.62 %, class A 5.2 %). The 600 s listing criterion is the upper bound of the extinction time at the MEC; at design concentration the build may extinguish at once (constructed).
3. Deep-seated and persistent sources: a class A cell that has charred, or an energised class C item, keeps its solid temperature; when C falls below the MEC it re-ignites if its surface is still above ignition. This is the mechanism NFPA 2001 annex text describes; the app has no measured re-ignition time.
4. Lithium-ion: the agent puts out flames but does not stop runaway inside the pack (water note, section 9); the pack re-ignites after the hold.
5. People: 30 s alarm-to-discharge delay after the confirming detector; occupants still in the room at discharge are exposed to C; flag C above 9.0 % (NOAEL) and exposures longer than the PBPK time above 10.5 %. HF warning (not a number) after any extinguished fire, stronger for liquid pools.
6. Warnings: enclosure integrity (doors held open by escaping occupants lower the concentration; not modelled), deep-seated fuels (paper storage, cable bundles), power left on above 480 V.

## 2. Where a clean agent is required or recommended

### 2.1 SBC 801-CR-2018 (read) and IFC 2021

- No clause of SBC 801-CR-2018 requires a clean agent in any room. Section 904 governs alternative systems where they are installed; 904.2 makes any system installed instead of required sprinklers subject to the official's approval, and 904.2.1 denies such systems the trade-offs allowed for sprinklers.
- 903.3.1.1.1 (exempt locations) is the clause that lets a sprinklered building leave a room without sprinklers, always with automatic fire detection per 907.2: (1) where water, or flame and water, is a serious life or fire hazard; (2) where the official approves that the contents make sprinklers undesirable; (3) generator and transformer rooms separated by 2 h walls and floor/ceiling or roof/ceiling assemblies; (4) noncombustible rooms with wholly noncombustible contents; (5) fire service access elevator machine rooms and machinery spaces; (6) the same for occupant evacuation elevators. Its preamble excludes omitting sprinklers merely because a room is damp, fire-resistance-rated or contains electrical equipment. An electrical or server room therefore keeps its sprinklers unless the official accepts item (1) or (2).
- 903.2 Exception: in telecommunications buildings, spaces used only for telecom equipment, its power distribution, batteries and standby engines need no sprinklers with smoke detection throughout and 1 h fire barriers (or 2 h horizontal assemblies).
- 904 numbering. SBC 801-CR-2018: 904.2 where permitted; 904.3.1 wiring; 904.3.2 automatic actuation with a manual means (it cross-refers to 904.11.1, a number left over from IFC 2015 where 904.11 was commercial cooking; in SBC 904.11 is water mist and the cooking manual station is 904.12.1); 904.3.3 interlocks; 904.3.4 alarms and warning signs; 904.3.5 monitoring by the building fire alarm system per NFPA 72; 904.4 acceptance; 904.5 wet chemical (NFPA 17A); 904.6 dry chemical (NFPA 17); 904.7 foam (NFPA 11 and NFPA 16; tests per NFPA 25); 904.8 CO2 (NFPA 12); 904.9 halon (NFPA 12A); 904.10 clean agent (NFPA 2001; tests at 12 months, containers at 6 months with refill above 5 % weight or 10 % pressure loss); 904.11 water mist (NFPA 750); 904.12 commercial cooking. IFC 2021 keeps 904.2 to 904.11 under the same numbers and continues with 904.12 aerosol (NFPA 2010), 904.13 commercial cooking and 904.14 domestic cooking; IFC 2024 inserts 904.12 hybrid systems (NFPA 770) and shifts the rest by one (agent; WAC 51-54A-0904 and state worksheets).

### 2.2 NFPA 75 and NFPA 76

- NFPA 75 has had fire protection in Chapter 9 since at least the 2017 edition (the 8.x numbers are from 2013). 2024: IT rooms in sprinklered buildings get sprinklers (9.1.1); in unsprinklered buildings sprinklers, a gaseous agent or both, which the Chapter 4 risk assessment may decide (9.1.1.1, 9.1.1.2); sprinklers per NFPA 13, valved separately with the valve outside the room (9.1.2, 9.1.3); a gaseous system protecting only the space below a raised floor must be CO2 or inert gas, and a clean agent above the floor must protect the underfloor at the same time (9.1.1.3 to 9.1.1.6). Gaseous total flooding should be considered as a supplement where data, equipment and return to service are critical, in sprinklered or unsprinklered rooms (9.4.1), designed to NFPA 12, 12A or 2001 with an agent that does not damage the IT systems (9.4.2, read in SR-8 of the 2020 cycle); power to electronic equipment is disconnected on gaseous discharge unless the risk assessment requires continuity (9.4.2.1). Portable extinguishers (2020 9.3, read): CO2 or halogenated agent for electronics, at least 2-A units for paper and plastics, dry chemical not permitted, a sign at each unit. Lithium-ion UPS in IT rooms: sprinklers (2020 11.5.4.7.1, extracting NFPA 1).
- NFPA 76 (telecommunications facilities), as gathered by the agent: very early warning smoke detection in signal-processing and main distribution frame areas where the signal-processing area exceeds 2500 ft2 [232 m2], early warning in power and cable-entrance areas (2024 6.8.6.1, 6.11.6.1, 6.10.5.1); sprinklers suit support, administration and building-service areas but are not recommended in power, main distribution frame, signal-processing or standby-engine areas, where double-interlock preaction reduces the risk (2020 A.8.6.2.2.1); no dry chemical or corrosive-liquid extinguishers in those areas (2024 8.6.3.1.3).

### 2.3 What this means for the room assignment

For a commercial building that is sprinklered, the code-consistent assignment for server, computer, data-processing, network and telecom equipment rooms and for electrical rooms is: sprinklers (preaction where water damage matters) plus HFC-227ea as a supplement (NFPA 75 9.4.1), with the sprinklers removed only where the official accepts 903.3.1.1.1 item (1) or (2), or the space is a telecom-only space under the 903.2 exception. Transformer rooms and generator rooms separated by 2 h assemblies with detection may drop sprinklers (item 3) and take a clean agent (transformer) or foam (generator; section 3). The model should make "clean agent with sprinklers" the default for these rooms and "clean agent only" a switch, and state which clause each choice rests on.

Diesel integral and sub-base tanks up to 660 gal [2500 L] indoors (NFPA 110 7.9.5; NFPA 37 6.3.2.2 in older editions); SBC 801-CR-2018 603.3.2.1 limits fuel oil inside a building to 2500 L in aggregate, or 11 350 L in protected above-ground tanks in a room sprinklered per 903.3.1.1. Engine rooms need 1 h construction (NFPA 37 4.1.2.1.1).
- Flammable-liquid storage rooms (SBC 5704.3.7): sprinklers per the liquid tables are the default, foam-water only where approved; NFPA 10 6.3.2 limits extinguishers to liquid surfaces of at most 10 ft2 [0.93 m2] as sole protection.

### 3.4 What foam does

NFPA 11 (A.1.1, as quoted in the 2024-cycle revisions) describes foam as a cohesive floating blanket that extinguishes by excluding air and cooling the fuel and prevents re-ignition by suppressing vapour. AFFF adds a surfactant film that drains from the bubbles and spreads over the fuel, sealing in vapour (MIL-F-24385F 3.3.1; NFPA 11-2021 Annex H draft); fluorine-free foams lack the film and rely on the blanket alone (Back and Farley 2020). Conroy and Ananth (2015, abstract) showed that contact with room-temperature foam cools the fuel surface in under 1 s and cuts its vapour pressure by more than 75 %: a conduction mechanism in the foam layer, not cooling by drained water alone. Hinnant, Giles and Ananth (2017) measured heptane transport through AFFF and fluorine-free foam layers (not read). The AFFF blanket drains in 10 to 15 min and must be renewed; SFFF blankets last 30 min to hours (NFPA 11-2021 Annex H draft).

Back and Farley define control as about a 90 % reduction of fire intensity, judged by the firefighter; Dahlbom et al. (2022) judge 90 % and 99 % control as 10 % and 1 % of the surface still burning (after EN 1568-3 and LASTFIRE). In both series control took about 40 % of the extinguishment time. The report's "L curves" show times rising steeply as the rate falls toward a critical rate at which the fire consumes foam as fast as it arrives: about 0.03 gpm/ft2 [1.2 L/(min m2)] for AR-AFFF at low expansion and 0.025 [1.0] at high expansion, 0.04 to 0.08 [1.6 to 3.3] for fluorine-free foams; the curves are worst-case envelopes, not fits. Back (2023) summarises that fluorine-free foams needed 1.5 to 3 times the rate and 2 to 7 times the extinguishment density of AFFF. Persson, Lönnermark and Persson (2003) modelled foam spread on large tanks and concluded that large pools need rates above the NFPA 11 minimum (abstract; their model neglects foam destruction by the fire).

### 3.6 Control and extinguishment times for a fixed system on a room-sized pool

No study measured a fixed foam-water sprinkler system on a room-sized pool at the NFPA rate. The transferable quantity in the tests is the foam solution delivered per unit pool area by control and by extinguishment, which for AR-AFFF on heptane was 0.9 to 1.2 L/m2 and 1.8 to 4.1 L/m2 (Back and Farley, Tables 9.1.1-2 and 9.2-1; derived), and for the fluorine-free foams 1.1 to 2.6 L/m2 and 2.2 to 7.3 L/m2. With t = D / R:

| Rate R | Foam | Control time t_c | Extinguishment time t_e |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.10 gpm/ft2 [4.1 L/(min m2)] (NFPA 11 spill, AFFF) | AFFF / AR-AFFF | 13 to 18 s | 27 to 60 s |
| 0.16 gpm/ft2 [6.5 L/(min m2)] (foam-water sprinklers) | AFFF / AR-AFFF | 8 to 11 s | 17 to 38 s |
| 0.16 gpm/ft2 [6.5 L/(min m2)] | fluorine-free | 10 to 24 s | 21 to 68 s |
| below about 1.2 L/(min m2) (AFFF) or 1.6 to 3.3 (FFF) | | control possible | no extinguishment |

These times are derived, not measured: they assume the delivered density at control and extinction does not depend on rate above the tested 1.2 to 1.6 L/(min m2), whereas the L curves flatten at high rates (a time floor set by spreading, which overhead sprinklers shorten because foam lands over the whole pool). They count from the moment foam solution reaches the pool, not from ignition or detection. Central values for the build: AFFF-type foam at 6.5 L/(min m2), control 10 s and extinguishment 30 s; at 4.1, 15 s and 45 s; fluorine-free foam, twice these; gasoline about 1.5 times heptane. A form consistent with the 90 % control definition is Q(t) = Q(t_f) exp(-(t - t_f) ln 10 / t_c) from foam arrival t_f, with the pool out at t_f + t_e (at t_e = 3 t_c the exponential is already at 0.1 %), and no re-ignition while foam is applied (10 or 15 min) plus the blanket's drain time (constructed). Water in foam-water sprinklers (94 to 97 % of the solution) also wets class A contents as an ordinary sprinkler would (water note, section 10).
