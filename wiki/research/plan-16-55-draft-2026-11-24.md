### 16.55 Smoke, CO, CO₂, O₂ and the asphyxiant dose: what is compared, and how (draft, 2026-11-24)

16.54 fixed the temperature quantities. This section takes the other four readings the egress takes at 1.8 m (soot and so visibility, CO, CO₂, O₂) and the asphyxiant dose made from them. It follows 16.54's forms: the suite's statistics, the project's band, the three readings, the strictest word, and for crossing times the four outcomes and the 5 % limit on misses.

**The plain finding first.** In the nine data files no smoke meter and no CO or CO₂ analyser stands between 1.5 and 2.0 m above the floor. One series has O₂ there (UL FSRI, 1.84 m). So, with the data in hand:
- O₂ can be compared at head height, in one series;
- soot, CO, CO₂ and the asphyxiant dose can be compared only under the ceiling, in two or three series from one laboratory (NIST), which is a weaker test of what the egress reads;
- none of the four can be called calibrated by the project's rule, which asks three series, whatever the numbers turn out to be. The most any can reach is indicative.

#### B. The comparisons

Common to all of them:
- **The two series.** The measured one at the test's own times. The model's at the same times, each value the mean of the model's 1 s readings within half the measured interval, as the suite does for a point device. The model is read in the device's own cell, the one the suite already uses; for the laser, the mean over the cells along its path.
- **Dry and wet.** The analysers of NIST/NRC and FSE read dry gas (SP 1013-1, section 8.1, p. 73: moisture and soot traps; the FSE input's devices have `DRY=.TRUE.`). The suite's O₂ and CO₂ pairs take the model's wet volume fraction and stay as they are. Each new quantity below is computed with the model both wet and dry (the wet fraction over one less water's), and the stricter word decides.
- **The statistic and the band.** The suite's δ and σ̃M, read the three ways (pooled, each series weighted alike, with and without the tests whose door the grid closes); calibrated needs δ within 0.8 to 1.25, σ̃M at most 0.35, and three series in band; indicative as 12.6 and the claim protocol have it; the strictest word decides.
- **Crossing times.** 16.54's rule: the first time at or above the level by a straight line between the bracketing samples; four outcomes (both cross: model time over measured time; model alone: an early closure; measurement alone: a miss; neither: set aside); pairs whose measured crossing is within three measured intervals of the start are left out; calibrated needs misses in at most 5 % of the devices where the measurement crosses.
- **Left out by rule.** Runs the health check stopped. Steady tests for any time. Pairs below the instrument, as 14.7 and 14.8 have it.
- **A check of each new reducer before it reads a result**, on made-up series with known answers, as 16.54 asks.

**5. The asphyxiant dose.** Needs no smoke decision. Under the ceiling only.
- **Where.** Only where CO, CO₂ and O₂ were measured at one point: NIST/NRC's upper sampling point (`O2-1`, `CO-3`, `CO2-4`, all at 6.85, 3.48, 3.22 m) and FSE's front and rear probes (2.08 m).
- **The dose.** The app's own (tenability.js `asphyxiantRate`): each minute adds CO in ppm over 35 000, times exp(CO₂ % / 5) when CO₂ is above 2 %, plus 1 / exp(8.13 − 0.54 × (20.9 − O₂ %)). Summed by the trapezium rule from the first sample at or after ignition, on the measured series and on the model's at the same times. A missing measured sample is bridged from its neighbours; a point with more than a fifth of its samples missing in the window is left out.
- **Two reductions.** `asphyxiantDose`: the dose at the run's end, model over measured, for points whose measured dose there is at least 0.03 (a tenth of the limit; a round figure fixed here so that no ratio of two noises is formed). `asphyxiantCrossing`: the time the dose first reaches 0.3, with the four outcomes and the 5 % rule.
- **Reported beside, model only:** at each tree's app cell, the dose at the run's end, the time to 0.3, and which of the app's four closures (60 °C, 10 m, the two doses) comes first.
- **The most it can say:** two series, one laboratory. And it inherits CO's caveat in full.

**6. What each smoke decision holds up.** Decisions 2 and 3 hold up comparison 1 and nothing else. Decisions 1 and 4 decide only whether a new series is prepared. Comparisons 2 to 5 need none.

#### C. The four smoke decisions, as proposals

The user has passed these four to the coordinator ("make them and compare", 2026-11-24). Each is written as a rule that can be checked. None was chosen by looking at how it would move a result: no model smoke value was read for this draft.

**Decision 1. The FAA cargo compartment series.**
- *The question.* Add it as a second smoke series, or leave it out as 12.9 and 13.2 did?
- *What the sources say.* A Boeing 707 hold, 6.7 by 3.2 by 1.37 m, a 0.1 by 0.1 m tray of plastic resin at 5 kW (FDS Validation Guide, section "FAA Cargo Compartments"). The input gives the fuel a soot yield of 0.126, a CO yield of 0.095 and a radiant fraction of 0.55, from cone data (`FAA_B707_Test_1.fds`). FDS ran it on cells of about 2 cm (160 cells across 3.18 m). Light transmission at 1.30, 1.02, 0.74 and 0.33 m. The meters' wavelength was not found: the FAA's report server did not answer on 2026-11-24.
- *The facts that decide.* The hold is 1.37 m tall, so no instrument is, or could be, at the 1.8 m the egress reads. The fire's D*/δx is 0.34, 0.43 and 0.58 on 0.34, 0.27 and 0.20 m cells (D* = 0.116 m for 5 kW), against a lowest value in the suite of 0.71, 0.89 and 1.20 (Steckler's 31.6 kW tests, from the saved 13.B files). The tray is a ninth to a quarter of one cell face. The hold is 4, 5 and 7 cells tall. Its walls are barrel-shaped.
- *Options.* (a) Leave it out. (b) Run it on 0.20 m only. (c) Run it on all three cells.
- *What each does.* (a) leaves smoke with its present series. (b) and (c) add a series whose fire is smaller than a cell and whose every reading is below head height; a second "series in band" from it would count toward a word about visibility at 1.8 m that it cannot support.
- **Proposed decision: (a), leave it out.** Rule: a series enters a tenability comparison only if its compartment holds the 1.8 m reading height and its fire's D*/δx on the cell is at least the lowest already in the suite on that cell (0.71, 0.89, 1.20). FAA cargo fails both.

**Decision 2. The heptane soot yield.**
- *The question.* Which soot yield does a heptane test take: the 0.0149 the suite has passed since 12.9, or another?
- *What the sources say.* NIST SP 1013-1, Table 3-3 (p. 16): heptane, soot yield 0.0149 ± 0.0033 (expanded), CO yield < 0.008, radiant fraction 0.44 ± 0.07, reproduced from Hamins et al. 2003b, measured for the NIST/NRC spray burner. The guide takes the yield's relative standard uncertainty as 11 % (Error chapter, "Smoke Obscuration"). The WTC inputs give 0.015 for heptane and 0.114 for the tests with the heptane and toluene blend. Other values exist in handbooks and in NIST's calorimetry records for heptane pools; none was opened and checked for this draft.
- *Options.* (a) Each series' own: 0.0149 for NIST/NRC and FSE, the WTC inputs' for WTC, as the data files carry now. (b) One handbook value for every heptane test. (c) A value chosen for each series from its burner (spray or pool).
- *What each does.* The model's soot density, and so K, is proportional to the yield. So the yield's ± 22 % passes straight into the obscuration's bias, and any other value would rescale every heptane smoke pair by one factor. That is why it must not be chosen after a result.
- **Proposed decision: (a).** Rule: a test's soot yield is the one its own report measured, or failing that the one its FDS input states; it is never changed after a smoke result is read; and its stated uncertainty (± 0.0033 for 0.0149) is printed beside every smoke bias. For any new heptane series without a measured yield (an EN 54 TF5 pan, say), 0.0149 is used and named as borrowed from another burner. A test with neither a measured nor a stated yield enters no smoke comparison.

**Decision 3. Scaling K_m to the instrument's wavelength (880 nm).**
- *The question.* The app's 8700 m²/kg is Mulholland and Croarkin's value for red light. When a smoke meter works at another wavelength, is the model's reading of that meter scaled?
- *What the sources say.* SP 1013-1 (section 5.1, p. 39): 8.7 m²/g "at a wavelength of 632.8 nm", and NIST/NRC's meter is a He-Ne laser (section 5.2), so no scaling arises there. The guide gives 8700 ± 1100 m²/kg (expanded). TN 1455-1 (section 4.5, pp. 84 to 85): the home tests' meters are laser diodes of 630 to 680 nm. The 880 nm instruments are the MIREX meters of the EN 54 test rooms (Börger et al. 2024, in the literature note of 2026-11-12, with a meter at 1.52 m; Gnendiger et al., arXiv 2306.16182, give the source's peak as 0.88 µm). That series is not in the data files. For soot agglomerates much smaller than the wavelength, absorption dominates and goes as one over the wavelength (the reasoning SP 1013-1 gives in section 5.1; a fitted exponent close to −1 is attributed to Widmann 2003, which was not opened for this draft).
- *Options.* (a) 8700 at every wavelength. (b) K_m(λ) = 8700 × 632.8 / λ, which is 6256 m²/kg at 880 nm. (c) A series' own measured K_m where it reports one. (d) Compare such meters only as ratios between stations.
- *What each does.* (b) lowers the model's reading of an 880 nm meter by 28 % against (a), for the same soot. It does not touch the app's visibility, which is for visible light.
- **Proposed decision: (c) where a series measured its own K_m at the meter's wavelength, else (b).** Rule: the model's reading of a meter at wavelength λ uses 8700 × 632.8 / λ m²/kg when λ lies outside 553 to 713 nm (632.8 nm ± 12.6 %, the ± 1100 of 8700), and 8700 inside it. So NIST/NRC (632.8 nm) and the 630 to 680 nm diodes take 8700, and an 880 nm meter takes 6256. The app's own constant stays 8700. **With the data in hand this changes nothing:** no meter in the nine files is at 880 nm. It binds the day an 880 nm series is prepared, and the exponent is checked in Widmann 2003 before then.

**Decision 4. NIST's smoke alarm tests.**
- *The question.* Add the eight flaming tests in the manufactured home as a smoke and CO series?
- *What the sources say.* They are the only series in NIST's repositories with smoke meters and CO, CO₂ and O₂ sampling at 1.5 m above the floor, which is where the egress reads (TN 1455-1, Tables 8 and 9). But the heat release was not measured: the guide fitted t² ramps in FDS to the fire room's highest thermocouple (section "NIST Smoke Alarm Experiments"). The soot yield was not measured: the input assumes 0.135 mol of soot per mol of fuel, about 0.010 kg/kg (computed in the literature note). The meters' uncertainty is 10 % of the reading, and they drift once hot gas reaches them (TN 1455-1, p. 86). The guide itself compares only the ceiling thermocouples and the alarms' times in this series.
- *Options.* (a) Leave it out, as 13.2 did. (b) Compare soot, visibility and CO at 1.5 m with FDS's fitted fire and assumed yields. (c) Compare only ratios between a remote station and the fire room at 1.5 m, in which the yield and K_m cancel, and arrival times.
- *What each does.* (b) would test the fitted fire and the assumed yields as much as the solver, and its head-height numbers would look like the missing evidence without being it. (c) removes the yield and K_m but still rests on a fire fitted with another model.
- **Proposed decision: (a) for every absolute comparison, with (c) left as a separate series to be prepared and pre-registered on its own, not tonight.** Rule: a series enters an absolute comparison of soot, visibility, CO or dose only if its heat release was measured (calorimetry or fuel flow) and its yield was measured or stated by its own report. The smoke alarm tests fail both. If (c) is prepared it counts toward "smoke transport for a given yield" only, and its section says so before any run.

#### D. What is stored, and the runs

**Stored.** The suite's `--store-species` (this branch, commit 0ca580c; `tools/calibration/lib/species.mjs`) keeps in each run, under `series.species`, each second from 1 s:
- `T` (K), `soot` (kg/m³, density times the soot mass fraction), `co`, `co2`, `o2`, `h2o` (mass fractions, the solver's units) and `mol` (the gas's moles per kg, so that a wet volume fraction is Y / M / mol and a dry one divides by one less water's);
- at each tree's head-height cells (the ones `series.head` keeps) and the cell the app reads at 1.8 m (`sites.trees[id].app`);
- at each gas analyser's cell and along each smoke meter's path, with the CO analysers of NIST/NRC and FSE that the data files now list under `species` for storing only.

It makes no pair and enters no statistic. With the switch off the output is unchanged, and with the 13.B flags one test reproduces the saved pairs and head series to the digits stored (the commit message and `benchmark/results/2026-11-24-species-check-*` hold the check).

- The machine has 28 logical processors (8 fast cores with two threads each and 12 slower ones). The 0.20 m job holds 8. A second suite at 8 jobs fits, but both then share the fast cores, so the factor is a guess and not a measurement. Storing itself costs nothing that shows: the small test took 11.9 s without it and 12.1 s with it.
- Memory, not processors, may be the limit: the handoff of 2026-11-24 records about 3 GB free in the evening. If it is short, `--jobs 6` or `--jobs 4`.
- **File size.** About 40 bytes for each cell and second. The eight series hold about 1.5 million cell-seconds on 0.34 m (UL FSRI's 91 tests are 1.1 million of them), so a file grows from about 25 MB to about 90 MB, and by a fifth more on 0.20 m.

#### E. What cannot be compared with the data in hand

- **Soot and visibility at head height.** No smoke meter is between 1.5 and 2.0 m in any of the nine series. Needed: a test with a measured heat release, a measured or reported soot yield, and a smoke meter at 1.5 to 2.0 m away from the plume.
- **CO at head height, CO₂ at head height, and so the asphyxiant dose at head height.** No such analyser in any of the nine series. Needed: the same, with CO, CO₂ and O₂ sampled at one point at 1.5 to 2.0 m.
- **CO when the fire is short of air,** as a test of the solver: the yield is a fixed input. FSE can show the gap; it cannot calibrate it. This waits for 16.53's proposal 4, the candidate item behind a switch.
- **The app's own fuels.** Their soot and CO yields are in no validation run: every test burns its own fuel. The chairs burn the app's class A fuel but measured no smoke and no CO.
- **The constants 3 and 8700 as what a person sees,** and the dose constants: no dataset here measures seeing or harm.
- **Irritants and HCN:** not modelled, so not compared.

Candidates that would give head-height smoke, each needing its own data file and its own section before any run: the EN 54 room of Börger et al. (a meter at 1.52 m, at 880 nm, heptane, so decisions 2 and 3 bind there); the ABS series with its measured K_m; the FPRF corridor tests; NIST's smoke alarm tests as ratios only (decision 4).

#### F. Open, unverified or doubtful in this draft

- The wavelength of the FAA cargo smoke meters, and that of FSE's `I/Io`, were not found.
- Widmann 2003 and Mulholland and Croarkin 2000 were not opened; their values are as SP 1013-1 and the guide quote them.
- WTC's CO column and its units were not verified, so WTC carries no CO.
- NIST/NRC's CO reads zero through five tests. Whether that is a reading or an idle channel was not established; the floors of comparison 2 leave those tests out either way.
- The factor for running beside the 0.20 m job is a guess.
- `--solver-rev 98ca598` with the switch gives the same pairs and head series as the working tree without either, on that one test; the solver's 70 files have the same digests both ways.
- The check of the 13.B flags was made on one test, Steckler's test 10 on 0.34 m, whose door the grid closes. It agrees with the corrected 13.B file and, as it must, not with the first 13.B file made before 13.16's correction.
