# A speed, accuracy and robustness benchmark for Topologic Studio Next

Gap LG5 of the 2026-10-27 gap analysis (`wiki/research/gap-analysis-2026-10-27/README.md`) found that navigation graphs derived from IFC are reported on each author's own buildings, with no shared benchmark set, no metric accuracy against geodesic ground truth and no robustness test on imperfect IFC. This folder is Studio's answer for its own pipeline: a fixed list of public models identified by checksum, one command that measures every computation of the browser core on them, accuracy metrics against references that do not depend on the code under test, and a stress and regression suite. It is meant to be re-run whenever the core changes (for example after the stair and vertical-core work now in progress), so that the numbers below can be replaced rather than trusted.

## Reproducing the results

1. `npm ci` in `topologicpy-web-frontend` (the tools load the browser modules from `src/core` and web-ifc's Node build from its `node_modules`). Tested with Node 24.21.
2. Download the models listed in `manifest.json` into one folder, keeping each entry's `path` (default `~/ifc-samples`, or set `BENCHMARK_SAMPLES`); the four shipped examples are read from `topologicpy-web-frontend/public/examples`. For the stress suite, also download web-ifc's test fixtures (the manifest's `fixtures` entry) into `more/web-ifc-fixtures`.
3. `node tools/benchmark.mjs --verify` checks every file's SHA-256 (a Windows checkout of the shipped examples is accepted with CRLF folded to LF).
4. `node tools/benchmark.mjs` runs the benchmark and writes `benchmark/results/<date>.json`; `node tools/benchmark.mjs --stress` runs the stress and regression suite and writes `benchmark/results/<date>-stress.json`. Both print the tables below; `--summary <file>` prints them again from a results file. `--only REGEX` selects models or cases, `--quick` takes one run per timing and fewer samples.
5. `node tools/output-hash.mjs` prints a fingerprint of every output on the corpus; run it before and after a change to the core and compare with `--diff`. This is how each optimisation below was shown to change no result.

The model files are public but their licences vary, so they are never copied into this repository. Each manifest entry gives the public source, a direct download URL, the SHA-256 of the published bytes, the git blob id (which matches the source repository's GitHub listing; all 38 were checked against it), the IFC schema and the size.

## The corpus

38 models: 19 from web-ifc's own test files (ThatOpen/engine_web-ifc, `tests/ifcfiles/public`, among them the KIT FZK-Haus and Institute, NIBS Office A, a dental clinic, a school and a 70 MB architectural model), 3 from youshengCode/IfcSampleFiles (two Revit IFC4 exports and a sample house), the two buildingSMART Simple Scene files (IFC4 and IFC4.3), 5 from andrewisen/bim-whale-ifc-samples, 5 from the xBim test files, and the 4 examples shipped with Studio (the NIBS Duplex, an earlier revision of buildingSMART's sample scene, and two CC0 buildings generated by `tools/make_example_ifcs.py`). Two xBim files, `LargeTriangulatedCoordinates` and `Axis2PlacementError`, test large coordinates and a broken placement rather than buildings, so they run in the stress suite only; 36 models enter the benchmark.

Two apparent duplicates were checked. youshengCode's `Ifc2x3_SampleCastle.ifc` is byte-identical to web-ifc's `schependomlaan.ifc` (same git blob) and is benchmarked once. bim-whale's `AdvancedProject.ifc` is not a copy of web-ifc's `advanced_model.ifc`: both are exports of a Revit advanced sample project, but on different dates (2020-11-30 and 2021-02-01), by different exporter versions, with 770,172 against 594,374 entities and 381 against 128 standard walls, so both are kept.

## What is measured, and why

Every computation is called as the app calls it, with the app's defaults (`src/state/useStudio.js`): a 0.5 m grid with the agent 0.75 m above the floor and at most 40,000 points; fire forecasts of 60 steps of 5 s, untenable from 80 model degrees; walking at 1.2 m/s; a dose limit of 0.3; reinforcement learning with 400 episodes of at most 200 steps, the fire on at one hop per step, walls on; three alternative routes. Extraction uses web-ifc's Node build through `tools/ifc-extract.mjs`, as the cross-model checks do; the app converts the same file with ThatOpen's importer in a worker, which is not timed here.

Samples are drawn with a generator seeded by the model's SHA-256, so a re-run on the same files draws the same starts, exits and pairs.

### Speed

Each model runs in its own Node process, one at a time, with a collection between timed runs. A timing is the median of up to five runs, stopped early once a computation has used 20 s on a model; per-route timings are the median over the model's sampled pairs. Timed per model:

- IFC extraction, geometry only and with the door flags and IFC semantics;
- the lattice build (`buildLatticeGraph`);
- A* (`astar`) and the user-facing route (`findRoute`, snapping included) for 20 start and exit pairs, the start a random floor node of a part that holds an exit and the exit one of that part's exits (random pairs where a model has no exits);
- Yen's alternatives (k = 3) and the app's default "distinct" alternatives, on 5 of those pairs, with the app's 5 s budget, counting the runs that hit it;
- straightening (`straightenRoute`) of every route, and once the per-graph lookups it builds;
- both fire forecasts (temperature and flood) and the hop-front surrogate (`hopSurrogate`), from a seed in the middle of the largest part;
- `planWithForecast`, `escapeDeadlines`, `doseDeadline` (tolerance 0.1 s, as the worker) from 5 starts spread over the nodes that can still leave, and `doseDeadlineMap` with its grid step, layer count and work (layers times links plus nodes);
- `trainRl` at the app's defaults on 3 pairs, including the exact optimum's backward pass, and with a single episode, which isolates the fixed cost;
- peak memory: the process's peak resident set after each phase (a phase cannot lower it, so a rise is that phase's).

The machine is recorded in each results file.

### Accuracy

**1. Route length against the geodesic.** For pairs of floor nodes on the same floor (the same 10 cm wall section), in the same part and at least 2 m apart, the exact shortest path in the free plane of that floor is computed independently of the graph: a shortest-path search over the visibility graph of the outline corners of the wall sections the lattice itself was blocked with (`sections`, cut 0.75 m above the floor), plus the two end points (`tools/geodesic.mjs`). Only bitangent links are tried, lazily, by A*, and only corners inside the ellipse |s c| + |c t| <= U are considered, with U the grid route's plan length. Visibility is decided permissively: a segment is blocked only if it crosses an outline segment at a point strictly inside both (the lattice's own test, `Segments.lean`), or if, between two consecutive outline points it touches, its middle lies inside solid by the lattice's own parity rule and more than 1 mm from every outline. An error can therefore only make the geodesic shorter, never longer, and cannot invent a violation. The stretch of the grid route (A*) and of the straightened route is their plan length over the geodesic's. Pairs whose route leaves the floor (by a stair or a ramp section) are counted and skipped.

Two results in `formal/fireegress` give the reference values. `Eikonal.lean` (`graph_front_never_early`, `geodDist_le_plLength`) proves that a route along links that are straight segments inside the free region is never shorter than the geodesic; a route that is shorter must use a link that is not free, and each such route is diagnosed link by link as a door link (never blocked by design) or a lattice link that clips solid, with a shortfall under 1e-5 m and every link free reported as rounding (node positions are float32). `Lattice.lean` (`K_bounds`, `ratio_bounds`) proves that on an open floor the 8-connected lattice's shortest path is less than 1.0824 times the straight line; with walls the bound does not apply, so the stretch above 1.0824 is reported for all pairs and separately for pairs that see each other.

**2. Exit identification.** Where a model flags exit doors itself (Pset_DoorCommon FireExit or IsExternal, the occurrence's value over its type's, Revit's IsFireExit, or an EXTERNAL space boundary: rule 1 of `tools/exits.mjs`), the two fallback rules are scored against those flags at the level of graph doors: rule 2 (walkable floor on one side only) and rule 3 (a room on exactly one side). Precision is the share of the doors a rule picks that are flagged, recall the share of flagged doors it picks. The flags are not ground truth about egress (an external door need not be an exit), but they are the model author's own statement.

**3. Graph quality.** Parts (with walls on), parts of 20 or more nodes, the largest part's share, and the share of nodes with a path to an exit. Floor nodes per square metre of walkable plan area, the area rasterised independently at 0.25 m from upward floor faces and room bottoms with the lattice's roof rule (a full 0.5 m lattice gives 4 per square metre, wall-face nodes add to it); coverage is the floor nodes, wall-face nodes excluded, times the grid cell, over that area. Stairs: staircases (connected stair nodes), stair ends linked to a floor out of all ends (`stats.stairEnds`), staircases whose floor links reach two storey levels, and, as `tools/core-check.mjs` reports it, how many storey levels the best-joined part spans and the share of floor in parts spanning two or more.

**4. Forecast and deadlines.** From each sampled start, whether the earliest safe arrival (time-dependent Dijkstra) equals the first point of the time-dose front, as `Forecast.front_head_earliest` says it must. The dose deadline map against the exact deadline at 12 sampled nodes: the exact deadline is bracketed to 0.02 s by bisection on the exact least-dose search, the map's departure must work in the exact model and never be later than the bracket's bad end, and the gap is the bracket's good end minus the map's value (median and maximum, in seconds and in grid steps). The hop-front surrogate (`src/core/surrogate.js`) against the temperature forecast: whether it is conservative (`compareForecasts`: no node closes later, no rate lower), how much earlier it closes the nodes the full model closes, how many nodes it closes that the full model never does, and how much earlier the hard deadlines become.

**5. Robustness.** For each model, what it lacks (floor slabs, rooms, walls, doors, stairs, exit flags) and what the pipeline then does (floors from rooms, no door nodes, the exit rule used, coarsening, split parts, unlinked stair ends), with the number of geometry errors web-ifc reported.

## The stress and regression suite

`node tools/benchmark.mjs --stress` (`tools/stress.mjs`) runs each case in its own Node process with a time limit, so a hang or a crash fails that case and not the suite. A case passes when every check holds. "xfail" marks a case whose fix is on a branch not yet merged (none at present: the review fixes it was written for have landed); "open" marks a weakness the suite found in code outside this work's scope, documented under Findings; "fixed?" would mark an expected failure that now passes.

- **Synthetic geometry** (35 cases): meshes built as `src/core/lattice.test.mjs` builds them: straight, L and U stairs with the landing as a stair element and as a separate slab, a spiral, quarter-turn winders, a terraced ramp of slabs 2.5 cm apart, ramps at 1:12 and 1:8, a flight whose bottom tread is a full riser above the floor and one whose gap exceeds the riser limit, a flight ending at a door with the upper slab stopping at the wall face and one ending at an outside door with no floor beyond, split levels joined by two and three steps, a mezzanine over a floor, a 5 cm wall with and without a door, a building turned 30 degrees, a round room of 48 wall segments, a doorway with no slab under it, corridors of 0.45 m (below the grid spacing) and 0.7 m, a 200 by 200 m floor, zero-area and repeated-vertex triangles, NaN and infinite coordinates, inverted winding, a scene 500 km from the origin, and a scene in millimetres. Each checks its own properties (each staircase joins the floors it spans with one link per end, floors connected where they should be and not through walls, the point budget) and then that the graph is sound (finite points, valid edges), that it was built within a stated budget, and that the routing, forecast and deadline pipeline on it produces no NaN.
- **IFC files**: web-ifc's 75 functional test fixtures (malformed input, boolean robustness, primitives and voids, schema aliases and others), seven broken files made on the spot from a real model (empty, truncated to 40%, truncated in the header, JSON and binary data named `.ifc`, an IFC-XML document, a zipped model), and every corpus model, through extraction, the graph and the pipeline. Expected: no crash, either an error or a sound (possibly empty) graph, an error a person can read, and a small file failing within 5 s.
- **Known bugs** (15 cases): each a minimal case that fails on the code before its fix and passes now, with the commit or review finding cited. Running the suite with `--core-rev <parent of the fix>` (which loads `src/core` from that commit) confirmed the failure for every case whose fix lies in `src/core`; the cases whose fix lies partly in `tools/ifc-extract.mjs` (roof slabs, vertical cores) are checked against the numbers `tools/core-check.mjs` and the IFC survey report before and after.

Commit 2bfd1ca of `feature/benchmark` (next/browser-core at 96edff1 with the three optimisations below), on an Intel Core i7-14700 (28 logical cores, 31.7 GB) under Windows 11, Node 24.21. The full results are `results/2026-10-28.json` (11.3 minutes) and `results/2026-10-28-stress.json`; the tables below are printed from them by `--summary`. 36 models enter the benchmark; 33 give a walkable graph (ISSUE_021 is a structural model, ManySimpleWalls has walls and no floor, and xBim's IFC4x3 test model has no building elements Studio walks on), and 28 of those have exits.

Over 640 pairs on 32 models, the grid route is a median 8.0% longer than the exact free-plane geodesic (90th percentile 49%), and the straightened route 2.9% (44%). The open-floor bound of 1.0824 holds for 280 of the 331 pairs that see each other; the 51 others pass walls, missing floor or removed nodes on their way, where the lattice is not the open lattice the bound is proved for. The large stretches (up to 10.5 on Office A, 8.5 on Ifc4_SampleHouse) are pairs that the free plane joins directly or nearly so while the graph does not: across space with no floor nodes (a void, a shaft, the outside), which the free plane does not exclude, or through solid it counts as free (overlapping wall meshes whose outlines cancel). There the geodesic is a loose lower bound rather than a walkable path; the free plane is built this way so that it can only shorten the geodesic.

No route is shorter than the geodesic through a lattice link that clips a wall, and none by rounding. One grid route and two straightened routes, all on ISSUE_126, are shorter, each through a door link that is not free in the wall section at 0.75 m. Door links are never blocked by walls, by design, so where the wall's section is closed at a door, or the door node stands off the opening, the link passes through solid. This is the case `Eikonal.lean` excludes by its hypothesis that every link is free; it is a property of the model and of the door rule, not a fault in the router. The check of the geodesic itself (a route with every link free can never be shorter) found three such cases in the first full run, which traced to the merging of outline end points in `tools/geodesic.mjs` and were fixed (be39a24); in this run there are none, and a search over 10,051 free grid routes on three models finds none either.

### Forecast and deadlines

On all 28 models with exits, from 5 starts and with both fire models (280 searches), the earliest safe arrival equals the first point of the time-dose front. At 672 sampled nodes the deadline map's departure works in the exact model and is never later than the exact deadline's bracket. The gap below the exact deadline has a median per model of 0.4 s (up to 17 grid steps), and reaches 40 s on AdvancedProject, where the work cap (review K2) sets a grid step of 0.6 s on a 41,578-node graph; one median is slightly negative (-0.01 s on IfcOpenHouse), within the 0.02 s width of the exact bracket. The hop-front surrogate is conservative on all 33 models (no node closes later, no rate is lower, no hard deadline is later); it is also much earlier than the heat-diffusion model, by a median of 0 to 276 s per model in the hard deadlines, because a node 80 model degrees hot under diffusion is reached late or never while the hop front reaches every node one link per step.

### Findings the suite leaves open

These are outside this work's scope (`lattice.js` and `tools/ifc-extract.mjs` were not to be changed here) and are marked "open" in the results:

1. A floor triangle with an infinite coordinate makes `buildLatticeGraph` sample an unbounded range: the case runs until it is killed. A wall vertex at infinity makes the dominant wall angle NaN and the graph empty. Skipping triangles with a non-finite vertex would fix both.
2. Geometry in millimetres (units not applied) makes the lattice sample the whole grid at 0.5 units before coarsening, 4 x 10^8 samples here, and fail with "Invalid array length". Coarsening from the bounding box before sampling, as the point budget already implies, would fix it.
3. Broken or non-IFC files reach the extraction unchecked. web-ifc then fails with raw WebAssembly traps ("memory access out of bounds", "Aborted()") or a TypeError, and on some 23-byte to 4 kB files only after 5 to 14 s. Checking the header (`ISO-10303-21;`, a FILE_SCHEMA, a DATA section) before parsing would give a readable error at once.

Corridors narrower than the grid spacing are lost when no lattice line falls inside them (0.45 m between lines: rooms apart; 0.7 m: joined), and metre thresholds do not scale with units; both are reported, not failed, since they follow from the grid and the units Studio assumes.

## Optimisations made from these results

The first runs showed where the time went: the dose deadline map was the slowest computation in the app (median 1.0 s, 5.6 s worst, before the review's work cap), RL training was dominated by its fixed cost (one episode took as long as 400 on the 70 MB model, and a CPU profile put 95% of it in the exact optimum's backward pass), and a profile of `doseDeadline` put a quarter of the time in the binary heap's `pop`, four fifths of that on two destructuring swaps. ## What remains slow, and why

- **The dose deadline map** (0.34 s median, 1.6 s worst) does work proportional to layers times links: up to 1.5 x 10^8 link updates under the cap. Its cost is now bounded by the cap rather than by the horizon, and the cap is bought with a coarser grid on large graphs, which is still conservative but widens the gap below the exact deadline (40 s at most, on AdvancedProject). Going further would need a different method, for example computing the map only where a deadline is wanted, not a faster loop.
- **IFC extraction with door flags** in the Node tools (38 s on the 70 MB model) reads each door's property sets through web-ifc's helper; the app does not use this path. A one-pass reading like `ifcSemantics` would remove it from the tools.
- **The lattice build** (2.7 s on the 70 MB model) is in `lattice.js`, which was out of scope while the stair and core work changed it.
- **Yen's alternatives** (159 ms worst) copy the link mask and allocate fresh search arrays for every spur; reusing them would help, but Yen is not slow enough to matter at present.
- **A broken file** can take web-ifc 5 to 14 s to fail (Findings, 3).

## Limits

- One machine, in Node: the app runs the same modules in browser workers, where timings differ, and converts IFC with ThatOpen's importer rather than the web-ifc calls timed here.
- Samples are small (20 route pairs, 5 plan starts, 12 map nodes and 20 geodesic pairs per model), drawn reproducibly but not exhaustively.
- The geodesic is a lower bound on the walkable geodesic: the free plane ignores floor edges, treats overlapping wall meshes as free where their outlines cancel, and accepts sight lines within a millimetre of solid. So stretches are upper bounds on the true stretch, and violations cannot be invented, only missed where a non-free link happens to be balanced by a shortcut.
- The exit flags are the model author's statement, not ground truth: an external door need not be an exit, and an exit need not be flagged.
- The fire models are graph relaxations whose heat is an index, not a measured gas temperature, so the doses and deadlines rank routes under the model and are not measured FED values; the 5 s step, the 80 degree threshold and the 0.3 dose limit are the app's defaults, not recommendations.
- The storey metric reads storey levels from floor slabs (`floorLevels`), so it is undefined on models whose floors are rooms only (the dental clinic, Office A).
- The graph counts change whenever the lattice, stair or core code changes: re-run the benchmark rather than quoting these numbers after such a change.
